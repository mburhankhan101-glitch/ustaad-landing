Type

Inter throughout, heavy for anything with a voice and semibold for anything that is a label. The one thing that matters: tight tracking. Inter at its default spacing reads as a browser default once it is 130px tall, which is this face's only real failure mode, so display sizes run at -0.045em and nothing large is ever left at normal spacing. Three display sizes and no more. Ten sizes is what makes a grid look unplanned.

The bubble

What the Wordle tile is to NYT Games, the OMR bubble is to this. Every entry-test student in Pakistan has filled thousands of them, it carries real feeling, and it is a circle, so it costs nothing to draw. Empty is a ring, filled is solid, and that is the whole vocabulary. Never put a gradient or a shadow on one; the entire system rests on these staying flat.

An answer row

The row is the unit a student reads, so it is also the unit that gets marked. In a reel the layout never changes between frames: only the marking does, which is what makes a pan read as a sheet being filled in rather than as three separate cards.

The highlight

The one line on a card that is the point of the card. It exists because a line that merely changes between two otherwise identical frames does not register: nothing tells the eye to look again. On a block of accent it does. Never more than one per card, or it stops meaning anything.

The end card

The only frame in a piece that asks for anything, so it carries the name, what the thing is, who it is for, and where to get it. Three proof lines is the most that can be read in the two or so seconds it is on screen. The bullet is a filled bubble, so even the list is made of the one object the system is built on. The name is centred; the lines are not, because centring each one leaves three ragged edges.

Two kinds of post

Reach carries no ask and is judged on shares and saves. Conversion carries the ask, goes out only when something is actually happening, and is judged on link taps and installs. The reference account's own numbers show the gap: the brand piece took 22.9K likes, the 75% off piece took 65. The second one is not a failure. Judge a conversion post on likes and you will conclude it was, and stop making them.
